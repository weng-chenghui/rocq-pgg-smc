# S_5 × S_5 recovery feasibility: when AG-code-on-a-curve fails and what does not

**Date:** 2026-05-16

This note addresses the feasibility of using `S_5 × S_5` as the covering deck
group in a multi-party-computation secret-sharing protocol for the AIP 2026
conference poster. The reasoning was derived during poster revisions on
2026-05-15 and 2026-05-16 and is preserved here for future reference.

The closing question of the conversation was: *given that `S_5 × S_5` does
not admit a low-genus connected AG-code-on-a-curve construction, is the
group still usable for recovery, and via what?* The three-part answer is
recorded below, followed by a comparison table and citations.

---

## 0. Primary correction (added 2026-05-17)

The original framing conflates three concerns which this revision
separates explicitly. None of the high-genus numbers below is a
security parameter.

1. **Security.** pgg-smc establishes privacy below the collusion
   threshold via random-walk mixing on the deck group's Cayley
   graph. The spectral inequality lives in
   `pgg-smc/security/pgg_mixing.v`, the entropy form in
   `pgg-smc/security/pgg_entropy_security.v`, and the
   Rayleigh-quotient witness for this group in
   `pgg-smc/instances/s5x5/s5x5_mixing.v`. Curve genus does NOT
   appear in any of these bounds. The numbers `g >= 173` and
   `g = 15,121` below are rigidity-side obstructions to a
   particular curve realisation, not privacy parameters.
2. **Algebraic rigidity.** The curve under the cover supplies
   uniqueness of the monodromy class (the framework's
   `algebraic_rigidity` argument). Section 1 below answers the
   rigidity-side question: can `S_5 × S_5` be the full
   automorphism group of a single CONNECTED curve at small genus?
   It cannot. This is independent of security.
3. **Recovery primitive.** Whether the secret-sharing scheme
   exploits curve geometry (AG-code on a curve) or not (sum-mod,
   Shamir) is a separate axis. The operational instance
   `s5x5_pile` uses `sum_mod_scheme` from
   `pgg-smc/reconstruct/covering_scheme.v`, so its recovery
   primitive does not consult any curve.

Furthermore, the framework already supports INTRANSITIVE
(disconnected) covers via `MultiCoveringData` in
`pgg-smc/reconstruct/multi_covering.v`. The operational
realisation in
`pgg-smc/instances/s5x5/rigidity_s5x5_instance.v` is two
disjoint copies of Bring's curve, each at genus 4, with
`S_5 × S_5` acting componentwise (one factor per copy). The
total genus is 8 and the maximum per-component genus is 4. The
Hurwitz lower bound `g >= 173` of Section 1 applies only to
CONNECTED Galois realisations and does not constrain this
disconnected operational realisation. See Section 2.5.

## 1. Why AG-code-on-a-curve is infeasible for S_5 × S_5

**Hurwitz's bound and its lower-bound consequence.**
Hurwitz's automorphism theorem (1893) states that for a compact Riemann
surface `C` of genus `g >= 2`, the automorphism group satisfies
`|Aut(C)| <= 84 (g - 1)`. Applied to `|S_5 × S_5| = 14400`, this gives
the lower bound `g >= 1 + 14400 / 84 = 172.43`, hence `g >= 173`. The bound
is a NECESSARY inequality for the group to be realisable as a deck group
on a connected curve. It is not a SUFFICIENT realisation: there is no
guarantee that the floor is achieved.

**Saturation requires the (2,3,7) Hurwitz signature.**
A Fuchsian signature `(p, q, r)` denotes a discrete group presentation
`<a, b, c | a^p = b^q = c^r = abc = 1>` used to construct Galois covers
of the Riemann sphere. The Hurwitz bound is saturated only when the
deck group is generated by a `(2, 3, 7)`-triple. Element orders in
`S_5 × S_5` are `lcm` of pairs from `{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}`, giving the set
`{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 12, 15, 20, 30}`. There is no element of order
7, so the `(2, 3, 7)` signature is inadmissible for `S_5 × S_5`. The
Hurwitz lower bound 173 is therefore unreachable.

**Smallest admissible Fuchsian signature.**
Enumerate triangular signatures `(p, q, r)` with `p, q, r` drawn from
the admissible-order set `{2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 12, 15, 20, 30}`, minimising
`(1 - 1/p - 1/q - 1/r)`. The minimum is attained at `(2, 4, 5)` with
`1/20`, giving `g >= 1 + 14400 / 40 = 361`. The result is conditional on
the existence of generators of orders `(2, 4, 5)` in `S_5 × S_5` whose
product equals the identity, which itself requires verification. The
relevant invariant is `sigma^0(G)`, the **strong symmetric genus** of
`G`: the genus of the minimal connected Galois cover with automorphism
group exactly `G`.

**Fiber product upper bound.**
The fiber product `Bring's ×_{P^1} Bring's` with cross-ratio-distinct
disjoint branch loci is a connected Galois cover of `P^1` with deck group
`S_5 × S_5`. Each Bring's cover of `P^1` has total ramification `R = 246`
from Riemann-Hurwitz `2 · 4 - 2 = 120 · (-2) + R`. Applied to the
compositum:

```
2g - 2 = 14400 · (-2) + (120 · 246 + 120 · 246)
       = -28800 + 59040
       = 30240
```

so `g = 15,121`. This is an upper bound on `sigma^0(S_5 × S_5)`. With
generic branch loci, `Aut(C) = S_5 × S_5` exactly. A naive choice such
as branch loci `{0, 1, infty}` and `{2, 3, 4}` admits a Möbius swap and
gives the wreath product `S_5 \wr Z/2` instead, so branch-locus choice
must be cross-ratio-distinct.

**Cryptographic verdict (revised 2026-05-17).**
The gap statement that follows applies ONLY if the recovery primitive
is an AG-code defined on the SAME connected curve that carries the
`S_5 × S_5` action. Under that binding, the strong-ramp identity
`t_T - t_k = 2g` from [Chen-Cramer 2006] forces a threshold gap of
`>= 346` cards (Hurwitz lower bound), more realistically `>= 722`
(admissible-signature bound) or `30,242` (fiber-product realisation),
and the secret splits into `2g` components. No explicit curve realising
`S_5 × S_5` at any genus in `[173, 15,121]` appears in the Conder
catalogue (https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~conder/) or the LMFDB
Higher-Genus database (https://www.lmfdb.org/HigherGenus/).

The verdict is therefore narrow: do not couple the recovery scheme to
a single connected `S_5 × S_5`-Galois curve. It does NOT say
`S_5 × S_5` is unusable as a deck group. pgg-smc's `s5x5_pile`
instance proves the contrary: it uses `sum_mod_scheme` (gap = 1) for
recovery and the two-disjoint-Bring's intransitive cover (Section 2.5)
for rigidity, with security from random-walk mixing on the deck group.

**Cryptographic verdict (original, kept for diff visibility).**
If you build an AG-code on a connected curve carrying `S_5 × S_5`, the

## 2. Surface AG codes (Couvreur and co-authors) -- a research direction, not feasible now

**The setup.**
Replace the disjoint union `C_1 \sqcup C_2` (a disconnected scheme,
discussed in earlier conversation iterations) with the Cartesian product
`C_1 \times C_2`, a connected 2-dimensional variety. For two copies of
Bring's curve, `S_5 × S_5` acts faithfully on `Bring's × Bring's`
componentwise. The variety is connected. The function space mixes the
two factors via the Künneth formula:

```
H^0(C_1 × C_2, p_1^* L_1 ⊗ p_2^* L_2)  =  H^0(C_1, L_1) ⊗ H^0(C_2, L_2)
```

so an encoded function on the surface is a tensor of one function from
each curve, not a disjoint pair.

**Why this avoids genus explosion.**
For `g_1 = g_2 = 4` the geometric genus of the surface is
`p_g(C_1 × C_2) = g_1 g_2 = 16` and the arithmetic genus is
`p_a(C_1 × C_2) = g_1 g_2 - g_1 - g_2 = 8` (Hartshorne convention).
Either invariant is far below the single-curve fiber-product value
`g = 15,121`. Geometrically, the surface is much smaller than the
connected high-genus cover.

**Why it is not yet feasible.**

1. **Surface AG-code theory exists** (Couvreur with various co-authors)
   but is less mature than curve AG codes. Chen-Cramer 2006's clean
   `t_T - t_k = 2g` strong-ramp identity does not have a comparable
   single-parameter surface analogue.
2. **Threshold and ramp parameters** for surface AG codes are described
   by the canonical divisor class `K_S`, intersection multiplicities
   `K_S · D` and `D^2`, and Hilbert polynomial coefficients. These are
   not the single integer `g` of the curve case. The "gap" is
   parameterised differently and depends on the divisor `D` as well as
   the surface.
3. **Decoding** for surface AG codes has list-decoding and unique-decoding
   algorithms (Couvreur and co-authors; Cascudo-Cramer-Xing-Yuan and
   related work) but with slower complexity and smaller radius than
   Reed-Solomon or curve AG decoding.
4. **No named `S_5 × S_5` surface-AG-code construction** appears in the
   cryptographic literature. The mathematical scaffolding exists. The
   specific protocol does not.

**Status.**
A genuine research direction. For the AIP 2026 poster, this is the
"Future Horizon" line. For the Coq framework, it is not implementable
in the current codebase, which has only single-curve `CoveringData`
and `MultiCoveringData` (disjoint-union) records.

## 2.5. Intransitive realisation: two disjoint Bring's curves (operational)

Section 1's Hurwitz bound `g >= 173` assumes the cover is CONNECTED
and the deck group acts faithfully and transitively on the fibre. The
pgg-smc framework relaxes this. The record `MultiCoveringData` in
`pgg-smc/reconstruct/multi_covering.v` represents a cover by a list
of connected components, with per-component Riemann-Hurwitz data
indexed by the component's sheet count (the orbit length under the
deck group), not by `#|G|`. For an intransitive cover the relevant
arithmetic constraint is per orbit.

The operational realisation of `S_5 × S_5` in pgg-smc's `s5x5_pile`
instance is two disjoint copies of Bring's curve. The first factor
of `S_5 × S_5` acts on the first Bring's component, the second
factor on the second component. Each component carries an
`S_5`-Galois action over `P^1` and has genus 4. Per-component
Riemann-Hurwitz at degree 5 gives `2 · 4 + 2 · 5 = 5 · 0 + R + 2`,
hence `R = 16` per component, consistent with the standard Bring's
ramification.

The resulting cover has:

- `mcd_total_sheets = 10` (two orbits of length 5)
- `mcd_total_genus = 4 + 4 = 8`
- `mcd_max_genus = 4`

This is recorded in
`pgg-smc/instances/s5x5/rigidity_s5x5_instance.v` as
`s5x5_multi_data`, with the verification lemma
`mcd_total_genus_s5x5_E : mcd_total_genus s5x5_multi_data = 8` and
the realisation axiom `s5x5_multi_realised`. The single-component
commitment `cd_genus = 173` in the same file is retained ONLY to
satisfy the framework's older `CoveringData` record. The
disconnected representation is what the protocol actually runs on.

The trade-off is faithfulness vs connectedness. On two disjoint
Bring's curves the action of `S_5 × S_5` is faithful on the disjoint
union but NOT faithful per component: each factor acts trivially on
the other component. The pgg-smc rigidity argument is consistent with
this. It bounds the deck group through the combined monodromy class
across components, not through the automorphism group of one
connected curve. The `g >= 173` Hurwitz floor of Section 1 therefore
does not bind here, because Hurwitz applies to a single connected
curve realising the FULL deck group as its automorphism group.

## 3. Other (k, T)-recovery schemes work for S_5 × S_5

The infeasibility above is narrow. Only the AG-code-exploiting-curve-geometry
construction fails for `S_5 × S_5`. Standard secret-sharing schemes work
for any deck group, because they do not consult the group during encoding.

Furthermore, in pgg-smc the SECURITY of every recovery scheme below
comes from the same source: random-walk mixing on the deck group's
Cayley graph, formalised in `pgg-smc/security/pgg_mixing.v` and
specialised to `S_5 × S_5` in
`pgg-smc/instances/s5x5/s5x5_mixing.v`. The curve, when one is used,
contributes algebraic rigidity, not privacy. Substituting one
recovery primitive for another therefore changes the threshold
parameters `t_k` and `t_T` but does not change the mixing-based
privacy bound.

**sum-mod-`N`.**
`(T-1, T)`-threshold, gap = 1. Shares are addends and the secret is the
sum modulo `N`. Privacy below `T`: any `T-1` shares are jointly uniform.
Recovery at `T`: all shares sum to `s`. This is what the current
`s5x5_pile` instance uses, via `sum_mod_scheme` in
`pgg-smc/reconstruct/covering_scheme.v`. No curve needed. The scheme
is independent of the covering deck group: `S_5 × S_5` enters only
through the deck permutation, not through the recovery primitive.

**Shamir (Reed-Solomon, genus-0 AG).**
`(T-1, T)`-threshold over `F_q` with `q >= T + 1`. Pick a degree-`(T-1)`
polynomial with the secret at `f(0)`. Shares are `f(α_i)` for distinct
`α_i`. Same `(T-1, T)` privacy and recovery as sum-mod,
deck-group-independent.

**Packed Shamir or ramp (Franklin-Yung 1992).**
`(k, T)`-threshold for arbitrary `k < T` with gap `T - k`. Use a
degree-`(k + m - 1)` polynomial with `m` secrets at distinguished slots
plus `k` random pad. Privacy: any `k` shares are jointly uniform.
Recovery: `t_T = k + m` shares without error correction, or
`t_T = k + m + 2e` with `e` error-correction symbols. This is the
genus-0 counterpart of the Chen-Cramer ramp on a curve. It works over
`F_q` with `q >= T + 1` and is deck-group-independent.

**Why the AG-code-on-a-curve direction is needed at all.**
Positive-genus AG-codes beat Reed-Solomon's `q >= T + 1` constraint.
A positive-genus AG-code can give the same gap with smaller `q`.
Asymptotically, codes with constant `q` exist for `q >= 49` and
parameters approaching the Singleton bound. The structural-alignment
benefit appears when the deck group equals the automorphism group of
the curve, so that the protocol's permutation symmetry aligns with the
code geometry. This is what makes Bring's curve special for `S_5`.

## 4. Comparison table

Gap denotes `t_T - t_k`, the privacy-to-recovery threshold difference.
Smaller gap means tighter recovery and lower per-card overhead.

| construction                          | gap                | needs curve?    | works for S_5 × S_5? |
|---------------------------------------|--------------------|-----------------|----------------------|
| sum-mod-N (genus-0, additive)         | 1                  | no              | yes                  |
| Shamir (genus-0 AG, Reed-Solomon)     | 1                  | no              | yes                  |
| Packed Shamir (genus-0 ramp)          | T - k              | no              | yes                  |
| Strong-ramp AG on curve, S_5 deck     | 2g = 8             | yes (Bring's)   | yes (g = 4)          |
| Strong-ramp AG on curve, S_5 × S_5    | 2g >= 346          | yes (g >= 173, 15,121 explicit) | no (no named curve) |
| Surface AG on C × C, S_5 × S_5        | K_S · D, D^2 based | yes (surface)   | research direction   |
| **pgg-smc s5x5_pile (operational)**   | **1** (sum-mod)    | yes (two disjoint Bring's, intransitive, total genus 8) | **yes** (componentwise) |

The last row is the actual operational configuration. Security is
random-walk mixing on `S_5 × S_5`, recovery is `sum_mod_scheme`,
and the two-Bring's cover supplies algebraic rigidity at total
genus 8 with maximum per-component genus 4. None of the high-genus
numbers from earlier rows is incurred.

## 5. Citations

Standardised format: `[Author Year]`, *Title*, Venue (URL when stable).

- **[Chen-Cramer 2006]**, *Algebraic Geometric Secret Sharing Schemes and
  Secure Multi-Party Computations over Small Fields*, CRYPTO 2006,
  LNCS 4117, pp. 521-536.

- **[Franklin-Yung 1992]**, *Communication Complexity of Secure
  Computation (Extended Abstract)*, 24th ACM STOC, pp. 699-710. The
  canonical packed-Shamir and ramp-scheme reference.

- **[Hurwitz 1893]**, *Über algebraische Gebilde mit eindeutigen
  Transformationen in sich*, Math. Ann. 41, pp. 403-442. The
  automorphism bound `|Aut(C)| <= 84 (g - 1)` for `g >= 2`.

- **[Greenberg 1974]**, *Maximal groups and signatures*, in *Discontinuous
  Groups and Riemann Surfaces*, Ann. of Math. Studies 79, pp. 207-226.
  Building on his 1960 *Conformal transformations of Riemann surfaces*
  (Amer. J. Math. 82). Together these establish that every finite group
  is `Aut(C)` for some compact Riemann surface.

- **[Conder catalogue]**, *Large group actions on compact Riemann surfaces
  of genus 2 to 301*, https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~conder/.
  `S_5 × S_5` is not listed.

- **[Paulhus 2022]**, *A database of group actions on Riemann surfaces*,
  LMFDB import paper,
  https://www.jenpaulhus.com/research/Paulhus-lmfdb2022.pdf.

- **[Couvreur with various co-authors]**, surface AG codes.
  Representative references: Couvreur, *Construction of rational surfaces
  yielding good codes*, Finite Fields Appl. 17, 2011; Couvreur and
  Randriambololona, *Algebraic geometry codes and some applications*,
  arXiv:2009.01281, 2020.

- **[Braden et al. 2023]**, *Bring's curve: old and new*, Eur. J. Math.,
  https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40879-023-00706-0. Covers
  the history (Bring 1786, Edge 1978) and modern status of Bring's curve.
