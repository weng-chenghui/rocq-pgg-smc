# Pivot: making the shuffle security-load-bearing for other groups (den Boer beyond cyclic)

Date: 2026-07-01

Research pivot prompted by the question: den Boer's five-card trick makes the shuffle THE
security mechanism because the secret is written positionally and the read-off (three
consecutive hearts) is exactly the invariant of the cyclic shuffle group. Can we drop AG codes
and sum-mod-N and instead find a positional/combinatorial recovery, analogous to
three-consecutive-hearts, that makes the shuffle security-load-bearing for OTHER finite groups
(S5, S5xS5, dicyclic Dic_3, and beyond)?

## What the verified sources already establish

The direct answer appears to be yes, and it is in print. Regular-polygon cards are den Boer's
cyclic trick lifted to Z_n, and dihedral cards lift it to a non-abelian group, with the shuffle
carrying the security in both. The abstract shuffle model (Koch-Walzer) says any subgroup with
the uniform distribution is a legitimate, physically implementable shuffle, and the
automorphism-shuffle line shows structured subgroups are realizable from pile-scramble shuffles.

## The core dichotomy: shuffle-matters versus AG code (linearity)

The conclusion this pivot arrives at: a shuffle-matters recovery and an AG-code (T,k) threshold
cannot live in the same scheme. It is worth stating the reason at the exact level of generality,
because it is sharper than "AG code" and it identifies precisely what must be given up.

### The culprit is linearity, not AG specifically

AG codes are linear, and the real theorem is about linear codes. In any linear scheme, a
coalition's information about the secret is the span of its share columns. A shuffle is a code
automorphism, a column permutation that preserves the code. Permutations preserve spans. So the
coalition's information is unchanged by any shuffle. The shuffle is a symmetry of the recovery
structure, and a symmetry cannot change what it preserves. AG is just the case the current
instance uses.

### The clean form: where does the privacy randomness live

Privacy means the coalition's view distribution is independent of the secret, and that always
needs a randomness source. The question is which source.

- In an AG or any linear (T,k) scheme, the source is the dealer's random coefficients. The dealer
  fixes the secret and randomizes the other message coordinates, and those act as a one-time pad
  that hides the secret from a small coalition. The shuffle is a redundant deterministic symmetry
  sitting on top of a scheme that is already private without it.
- In den Boer there are no dealer coefficients. The arrangement is a deterministic function of the
  inputs. The cyclic cut is the only randomness, so the cut is what makes the revealed view
  independent of the inputs. The shuffle is the randomness source.

So "shuffling matters" is exactly "the shuffle is the privacy randomness source." That is the
whole distinction.

### Why a linear scheme can never hand that job to the shuffle

Even if the dealer randomness were stripped out to try to force the shuffle to carry privacy, it
still would not work in a linear scheme. The coalition's information is span-based, and any
permutation preserves spans, so a deterministic linear encoding plus a shuffle leaks exactly what
the raw columns leak. The shuffle has no room to inject uncertainty. Nonlinearity is what lets a
shuffle be the randomness source, which is why den Boer's read-off (three-consecutive-hearts) is
nonlinear.

### The conclusion, confirmed and bounded

Any (T,k) threshold built on an AG code, or on any linear code, cannot make shuffling matter. The
shuffle in such a scheme is inert by construction. To make the shuffle matter one has to leave
linear codes entirely and go to a nonlinear, shuffle-is-the-randomness scheme, which is the den
Boer model. And that model, so far, only delivers the degenerate threshold: privacy by scrambling
a full reveal, one card private, reconstruct from all. Whether a genuine (t, r<n) ramp can be
built in the nonlinear shuffle-randomness model is the one open question, and it is the only way
the two could ever meet.

So the dichotomy is real and forced:

- AG or linear (t,r) ramp, and the shuffle is inert. This is the verified Dic_3 result.
- Nonlinear, shuffle-is-the-randomness, and the shuffle matters, but the code is lost and only the
  degenerate threshold remains. This is den Boer.

There cannot be an AG ramp and a load-bearing shuffle in the same scheme. The trade is pinned
exactly.
